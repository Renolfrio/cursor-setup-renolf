## 1. Purpose

This playbook provides a structured framework for building an effective LinkedIn organic content strategy for B2B SaaS companies. It is based on research collected from ten experienced marketers, founders, and content strategists who actively share practical insights through LinkedIn, YouTube, blogs, and podcasts.

The objective of this playbook is to transform research into a practical Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) that marketing teams, founders, and content creators can follow consistently.

Rather than copying one expert's methodology, this playbook combines multiple perspectives, compares different approaches, and highlights the strategies that appear most reliable across various sources.

## 3. Executive Summary

LinkedIn has become one of the most effective organic channels for B2B SaaS companies to build trust, generate qualified leads, and establish authority without relying solely on paid advertising.

After analyzing content from ten experienced practitioners, several recurring themes emerged. Successful LinkedIn strategies are built on clear positioning, audience-first messaging, consistent publishing, and effective content distribution. While each expert has a unique perspective, the research shows strong agreement that sustainable growth comes from delivering valuable insights rather than promotional content.

This playbook combines those shared principles into a practical operating framework. It is designed to help founders, marketers, and content creators build a repeatable LinkedIn content system that emphasizes quality, consistency, and long-term audience trust.

# 4. Core Principles

## Principle 1 — Audience First, Product Second

### Why It Matters

The strongest content consistently focuses on solving audience problems before promoting products or services. Building trust starts with understanding customer pain points and providing useful insights.

### Practical Recommendation

- Identify the most common customer challenges.
- Create educational content before promotional content.
- Focus on solving problems rather than selling features.

### Sources

- Ash Read — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)
- Adam Erhart — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

---

## Principle 2 — Build Clear Positioning

### Why It Matters

Clear positioning helps potential customers immediately understand who you help, what problem you solve, and why your solution is different.

### Practical Recommendation

- Define a specific target audience.
- Communicate one clear value proposition.
- Avoid trying to appeal to everyone.

### Sources

- Adam Erhart — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)
- Alex Hormozi — LinkedIn Content (June 2026)

---

## Principle 3 — Publish Consistently

### Why It Matters

Consistent publishing increases familiarity and strengthens long-term audience trust. Sustainable growth is achieved through regular, valuable content rather than occasional viral posts.

### Practical Recommendation

- Create a realistic publishing schedule.
- Prioritize consistency over frequency.
- Measure progress over months rather than days.

### Sources

- Justin Welsh — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)
- Ash Read — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

---

## Principle 4 — Prioritize Content Distribution

### Why It Matters

Creating excellent content alone is not enough. Successful marketers maximize the reach of every piece of content through intentional distribution.

### Practical Recommendation

- Repurpose content into multiple formats.
- Share valuable content across relevant platforms.
- Treat distribution as part of the content creation process.

### Sources

- Ross Simmonds — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)
- Gael Breton — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

---

## Principle 5 — Measure, Learn, and Improve

### Why It Matters

Content strategy should evolve based on performance data and audience feedback rather than assumptions.

### Practical Recommendation

- Track engagement and audience growth.
- Review high-performing content regularly.
- Improve future content using previous insights.

### Sources

- Brian Dean — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)
- Rand Fishkin — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

The recommendations in this document are supported by the research collected throughout this project and are intended to serve as a practical reference rather than a rigid set of rules.

# 5. Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

The following Standard Operating Procedure combines recurring recommendations from the ten experts into a practical workflow for building a LinkedIn organic content strategy for B2B SaaS.

---

## Step 1 — Define Your Target Audience

### Objective

Clearly identify the audience you want to reach before creating content.

### Actions

- Define your ideal customer profile (ICP).
- Identify their biggest challenges.
- List the questions they frequently ask.
- Understand the outcomes they want to achieve.

### Why This Matters

Content performs better when it solves specific problems for a clearly defined audience.

### Sources

- Adam Erhart — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)
- Rand Fishkin — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

---

## Step 2 — Build a Clear Positioning Statement

### Objective

Communicate exactly who you help, what problem you solve, and why your solution is different.

### Actions

- Write one simple positioning statement.
- Avoid generic messaging.
- Focus on customer outcomes instead of product features.

### Why This Matters

Clear positioning increases relevance and improves audience understanding.

### Sources

- Adam Erhart
- Alex Hormozi

---

## Step 3 — Create Valuable Content

### Objective

Produce educational content that helps your audience before asking them to buy.

### Actions

- Share practical advice.
- Explain common mistakes.
- Publish lessons learned.
- Answer frequently asked questions.
- Tell real stories and case studies.

### Why This Matters

Helpful content builds trust and authority over time.

### Sources

- Justin Welsh
- Ash Read

---

## Step 4 — Publish Consistently

### Objective

Maintain a consistent publishing schedule.

### Actions

- Choose a realistic posting frequency.
- Maintain quality across every post.
- Build a repeatable publishing process.

### Why This Matters

Consistency creates familiarity and long-term audience growth.

### Sources

- Justin Welsh
- Ash Read

---

## Step 5 — Distribute Every Piece of Content

### Objective

Increase the reach of every published post.

### Actions

- Repurpose successful posts.
- Share content across multiple relevant channels.
- Encourage discussions through comments.

### Why This Matters

Distribution multiplies the value of every content asset.

### Sources

- Ross Simmonds
- Gael Breton

---

## Step 6 — Measure Performance

### Objective

Continuously improve your strategy using performance data.

### Actions

- Track engagement.
- Identify top-performing content.
- Analyze audience feedback.
- Improve future posts using previous results.

### Why This Matters

Continuous improvement creates sustainable long-term growth.

### Sources

- Brian Dean
- Sam Oh

# 6. Where Experts Disagree

Different experts often approach B2B SaaS content strategy from different perspectives. Rather than treating these differences as contradictions, they can be viewed as strategic choices depending on business goals.

---

## Example 1 — Posting Frequency vs. Content Quality

### Justin Welsh

Justin Welsh emphasizes consistent publishing to build visibility and trust over time. His content frequently encourages creators to develop a repeatable publishing habit.

**Source:** Justin Welsh — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

### Ash Read

Ash Read focuses more on delivering consistently valuable content than on maintaining a specific posting frequency. His work emphasizes relevance and usefulness over volume.

**Source:** Ash Read — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

### My Decision

I recommend prioritizing quality while maintaining a realistic publishing schedule (for example, 3–5 posts per week). Consistency is important, but publishing low-value content simply to meet a schedule can reduce audience trust.

---

## Example 2 — Content Creation vs. Content Distribution

### Ross Simmonds

Ross Simmonds strongly advocates for investing in content distribution. He argues that creating great content is only half the job, and marketers should dedicate significant effort to amplifying its reach.

**Source:** Ross Simmonds — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

### Brian Dean

Brian Dean places stronger emphasis on producing high-quality, comprehensive content that earns attention through its value.

**Source:** Brian Dean — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

### My Decision

I believe both activities are essential, but I would prioritize creating high-quality content first. Strong distribution cannot compensate for weak content, while valuable content provides a solid foundation for effective distribution.

---

## Example 3 — Messaging vs. Search Intent

### Alex Hormozi

Alex Hormozi focuses on clear messaging, compelling offers, and communicating customer outcomes.

**Source:** Alex Hormozi — LinkedIn Content (June 2026)

### Sam Oh

Sam Oh emphasizes understanding search intent and creating content that answers the specific questions audiences are actively searching for.

**Source:** Sam Oh — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

### My Decision

For LinkedIn organic strategy, I would begin with strong messaging because users are not searching in the same way they do on Google. However, understanding audience intent remains valuable when selecting content topics.

# 7. What I Rejected and Why

Not every recommendation from the research was included in this playbook. Some ideas may work in specific situations but are less suitable for a practical LinkedIn organic strategy for most B2B SaaS companies.

---

## Rejected Idea 1 — Publish Every Day

### Source

Justin Welsh — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

### Why I Rejected It

Daily publishing can increase visibility, but it may not be realistic for small teams or solo founders with limited resources. I believe maintaining high-quality content on a sustainable schedule is more effective than prioritizing posting frequency alone.

---

## Rejected Idea 2 — Create Long-Form Content for Every Topic

### Source

Brian Dean — LinkedIn Posts (June 2026)

### Why I Rejected It

Long-form educational content can provide significant value, but not every LinkedIn topic requires an in-depth explanation. Short, focused posts are often more suitable for LinkedIn's fast-moving content environment. The format should match the audience's needs rather than follow a single content length.

# 8. My Original Ideas

## Idea — Build a "Question Bank" Before Building a Content Calendar

### The Idea

Instead of starting with a monthly content calendar, I recommend building a centralized "Question Bank." This document collects recurring questions from sales calls, customer support conversations, product demos, LinkedIn comments, and community discussions.

Rather than brainstorming random post ideas, each LinkedIn post should answer one real question from this bank.

### Why I Think It Could Work

Most successful B2B SaaS content solves real customer problems. A Question Bank keeps content closely aligned with actual audience needs instead of assumptions or trends.

This approach also creates a repeatable workflow:

1. Collect customer questions continuously.
2. Organize them by topic.
3. Prioritize the most frequently asked questions.
4. Turn each question into one educational LinkedIn post.
5. Review engagement to identify which topics generate the most value.

Although I did not find this exact workflow described by any single expert in my research, it combines audience-first thinking with a structured content creation process. I believe it can improve both content relevance and long-term consistency.

# 9. Weaknesses of This Playbook

No playbook is universally applicable, and this document has several limitations that should be acknowledged.

## Limited Scope

This playbook focuses specifically on LinkedIn organic content strategy for B2B SaaS companies. Some recommendations may not apply to B2C businesses, e-commerce brands, or other industries.

## Based on Public Educational Content

The recommendations are based on publicly available posts, videos, blogs, and interviews. They do not include private business data or internal company strategies that may influence decision-making.

## Platform Changes

LinkedIn's algorithm and best practices continue to evolve. Strategies that perform well today may require adjustment as the platform changes.

## Individual Context Matters

Business size, audience, available resources, and marketing goals vary significantly. Organizations should adapt this playbook to their own circumstances rather than following every recommendation exactly.

## Limited Research Sample

Although ten experienced experts were included, the research does not represent every perspective in B2B SaaS marketing. Additional experts may provide valuable alternative approaches.

# 10. Who I Would NOT Recommend Following and Why

Among the ten experts included in this research, I would be less likely to recommend **Nathan Latka** for someone whose primary goal is learning LinkedIn organic content strategy.

Nathan Latka provides valuable insights into SaaS businesses, founder interviews, and company growth. However, much of his content focuses on business metrics, acquisitions, and SaaS operations rather than practical frameworks for building a consistent LinkedIn content strategy.

For marketers specifically looking to improve LinkedIn organic growth, I believe experts such as Justin Welsh, Ross Simmonds, Ash Read, and Adam Erhart offer more directly applicable guidance.

This does not diminish Nathan Latka's expertise; rather, it reflects the narrower scope and objectives of this playbook.
